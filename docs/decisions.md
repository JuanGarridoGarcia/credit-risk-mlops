# Decision log

Every design decision of the project: when, what, which alternatives were considered and why this one was chosen. All decisions are taken by Juan; Claude proposes and debates. Newest last.

| ID | Date | Step | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-001 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | The user is an **investor who decides whether to buy each loan when it is listed** | Lender deciding whether to approve the application; portfolio valuation (expected loss of the book) | `grade` and the interest rate are set by Lending Club and are visible to an investor at listing, so the features fit this decision. As a lender we would be using the lender's own score to decide, and rejected applications are not in the data |
| D-002 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | Production includes **PD, LGD and Expected Loss**; the API returns all three | PD only, LGD and EL later as a batch extension | The investor needs to know how much would be lost, not only whether the loan defaults: a high-PD loan at a high rate can still be worth buying. PD only was proposed to limit scope and withdrawn |
| D-003 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | PD target = **default at any point in the loan's life**; the **default rate within 12 months** is used as an early-warning metric in monitoring | 12-month default (Basel horizon) as the target | The Basel 12-month horizon serves a bank's annual capital. A buy-and-hold investor loses whatever defaults over the whole life (IFRS 9 uses lifetime loss once risk has increased). Lifetime labels arrive 3 to 5 years late, so a 12-month signal is needed to monitor in time. Juan first chose the 12-month target; Claude corrected its own recommendation after Juan chose D-001. The early-warning metric depends on the data having a default date (checked in step 0.2) |
| D-004 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | PD baseline = **logistic regression on `grade` only** | Constant default rate; the research-phase model | Answers whether the model knows more than the grade the investor already sees for free. Known risk: the gain may be small because `grade` dominates the PD |
| D-005 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | LGD baseline = **mean LGD of the defaulted training loans of each grade**; the global mean is reported as a reference | Global mean LGD (constant) | Same question as D-004, so the whole baseline EL is "what an investor can compute from the grade alone". The constant is kept as a reference to show how much the grade adds on its own |
| D-006 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | Model metrics: PD by **AUC (and Gini), PR-AUC, Brier score and calibration by segment**; LGD by **RMSE, MAE and mean predicted vs observed by segment**; EL by **EL / realised loss** | AUC only for the PD; recall as the PD metric | EL multiplies the PD, so the PD must be a real probability, not only a good ranking. Juan wants to study calibration further when it is built. Gini and PR-AUC are added: Gini is how credit teams report AUC, and PR-AUC measures performance on the minority class. Recall was discussed (Juan's concern: defaulters slipping through) and rejected as the model metric: it needs a threshold, and the model outputs a probability; recall alone is maximised by buying nothing; the asymmetric cost of the two errors is measured in money by the business metric (D-007). Recall, precision and the confusion matrix are reported in F3 at the buy-rule threshold |
| D-007 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | Business metric: with the **same budget**, realised default rate, realised loss and **realised net return** of the portfolio bought by the model vs the one bought by the baseline | Default rate and loss only | Net return is what an investor ultimately cares about. Step 0.2 confirms the data supports it |
| D-008 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | **Single prediction path: the API** (single loan and batch endpoint), with every prediction stored. In the simulation, a replayer sends each month's loans to the API. A monthly job only monitors and decides retraining. The held portfolio is re-scored only when the champion model changes | API for single loans plus a monthly batch that scores every loan of the month | Juan noticed that scoring the same loans twice is redundant. The investor decides at listing time, so one path is realistic, removes the risk of two implementations disagreeing and load-tests the API. Cost: a batch endpoint so the replay does not take hours |
| D-009 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | Constraints: explanation of every prediction (SHAP), API p95 below 200 ms, close to zero cost until AWS, review of `addr_state` and zip code as possible proxies for protected attributes, no information from the future | | Credit scoring is high-risk under the EU AI Act; a slow score is useless at listing; personal project; indirect discrimination is illegal in credit |
| D-010 | 2026-11-20 | 0.1 | The exact **buy rule** is decided in F3 | Fix it now | It needs trained models and the noise of their metrics to set a threshold that makes sense. What is fixed now is how the rule is judged (D-007) |
| D-011 | 2026-11-20 | 1.4 (advanced) | This decision log is created in the F0 pull request, ahead of step 1.4 | Create it in F1 as the playbook orders | So that the decisions of F0 are recorded from day one. Step 1.4 does not depend on anything pending, which is the playbook's condition for advancing a step |
| D-012 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | Data source: Kaggle `wordsforthewise/lending-club`, file `accepted_2007_to_2018Q4.csv.gz` (2,260,668 loans, 2007 to 2018), pinned by SHA-256; column meanings from Lending Club's official dictionary (Kaggle `jonchan2003/lending-club-data-dictionary`) | The cleaned research-phase dataset (`lending-club-loan-data-cleared`) | The cleaned dataset has no last payment date, so neither the label delay nor the 12-month early-warning metric (D-003) could be built. The full file has every column, including the payment fields needed for LGD and net return (D-007). Audit: `docs/data_audit.md` |
| D-013 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | PD label: Fully Paid = 0; Charged Off and Default (121+ days late, 40 loans) = 1; Current, In Grace Period and Late = no label (still running). The 2,749 "does not meet the credit policy" loans (2007 to 2010) are **removed** | Keep them with a flag (Claude's recommendation) | Juan: they were approved under an older, looser policy, so they would muddy the data, and a user of the API expects scores under the current policy. Cost: if the first training is in 2014-01, it loses 2,230 of its 14,308 matured loans (bad rate 27% vs 14% for the rest), which is taken into account in decision 9.5 |
| D-014 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | A column can be a feature only if it is **proven** visible to the investor at listing, i.e. it appears in Lending Club's Browse Notes dictionary (`reports/data_audit/browse_notes_fields.md`). Dropped: the 32 post-issue columns, `funded_amnt_inv`, identifiers, `policy_code` and `mths_since_recent_bc_dlq` (likely available, not provable). `issue_d` only orders and splits in time; payments and recoveries only build targets | Keep `mths_since_recent_bc_dlq` as probably available | Juan: if it cannot be proven it stays out completely. A leak makes validation look good and production fail. Free text (`emp_title`) is discussed separately |
| D-015 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | Free text columns (`emp_title`, `desc`, `title`) are **not used in v1**. A grouped `emp_title` (occupation or sector) may be tested later as a challenger in F3: it enters only if it beats the champion by more than the metric noise and passes a bias review | Group `emp_title` and use it in v1 | Income is already covered by `annual_inc`, `verification_status`, `dti`, `installment` and the joint fields. `emp_title` has 411,811 distinct values after lowercasing; the top 1,000 cover only 48% of loans, so grouping is a sub-project with its own error. Job title is a strong proxy for sex. `desc` stops in 2014 |
| D-016 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | A model uses only columns filled throughout its training window. A column reported only from a given date (bureau fields 2012 and late 2015, second applicant 2017) enters at a retraining, logged as a "new data field" event. When exactly it enters is decided when the retraining policy is designed (proposal to discuss: a challenger trained on the shorter window that includes the new field, promoted only if it beats the champion by more than the metric noise) | Use the columns from day one, with missing as a value | Missingness before the start date encodes the vintage, not the borrower (2007 to 2008 loans defaulted 21% to 26% and all lack `tot_cur_bal`). In production the field is never missing, so the learned rule would be useless. Fields legitimately empty (second applicant on individual loans) are handled with a joint-application flag |
| D-017 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | LGD is trained only on loans charged off **at least 12 months before** the training date | Use every charged-off loan; wait 24 or 36 months | Recoveries arrive over time and the data has only the 2019 total, with no dates. Net recovery (share of funded amount): 2.7% at 0 to 5 months after charge-off, 6.0% at 6 to 11, 7.1% at 12 to 23, about 8.5% at 36 to 59. At 12 months most of it has arrived; waiting longer delays LGD labels by years for 1 to 2 points. Older charge-offs are also from another collection era, which a single snapshot cannot separate. Known limitation: amounts are as of 2019, a small unavoidable look-ahead in the simulation |
| D-018 | 2026-11-21 | 0.2 | `addr_state` and `zip_code` are **not model features**. `addr_state` is kept only to monitor fairness: at each evaluation, performance and buy rates are compared across states to detect another feature acting as a proxy | Keep the state and drop the zip code; keep both | Both are proxies for race (redlining), illegal in credit even if race is never seen; credit scoring is high-risk under the EU AI Act. Gain over `sub_grade` on an out-of-time test (train 2012 to 2014, test 2015): state +0.0021 AUC (95% CI +0.0016 to +0.0028), below the research-phase noise of 0.003; zip prefix -0.0003 |
